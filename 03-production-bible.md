### 1.2 The two script templates [DATA]

Ollie rotates between two formats, and both have produced outliers.

#### Template A: "Cinematic Secret Thriller"
*Used in: Lottery (170K), Holding Co (111K), Second Income (160K, mixed)*

- 2nd person, present tense. Feels like a movie.
- A **ticking-clock** first scene: "You have a decision to make in the next 60 seconds" / "in 11 minutes a wire is scheduled…"
- **Time-jump lines** start paragraphs: "Three weeks in…", "4 months in…", "6 months in…", "18 months in…" (the lottery script has 10 of them)
- A side character with a first name becomes a **threat** (Dana, who guesses the lottery secret)
- The finance concepts are legal and structural: blind trust, LLC, holding entity, lump sum vs annuity, muni bonds
- **Ending: a moral dilemma as an either/or question, with no like/subscribe:**
  > "…would you finally tell them the truth? Or would you let the silence protect you for one more year?"

#### Template B: "Grounded Math Story"
*Used in: Portfolio (140K), 7 Figures (15K), Stopped Looking Rich (10K), Old Money, Track Every Dollar*

- Opens on the **same template scene**: "It's 7:41 on a Tuesday morning and you're standing in the break room…" A coworker makes a small comment ("Still doing the oatmeal thing?" / "Rough month?")
- **Spoken section labels**, short title-like sentences that act as chapters:
  - "Level one, the starting number." → "Level two, the person who explained it." → "Level three, the boring years." → "Level four, the temptation to stop." → "Level five, the first setback." → "Level seven, the crossover."
  - Or without numbers: "The starting number." / "The boring years." / "The first time the math becomes visible." / "The choice nobody saw." / "The crossover nobody announces."
- Exact salaries, rents, car prices, weekly contributions, and market dips ($2,890 every 2 weeks, $75/week = $3,900/yr, a $4,000 drop over 2 months, $140/week = $7,280/yr)
- A **credibility line**: "Here's where the story usually gets exaggerated in most videos. So it's worth being precise instead."
- **Bookend ending:** go back to the opening scene years later (the same break room, the same coworker, the same line), then the standard CTA:
  > "If this story made you think differently about ___, hit the like button and subscribe for more stories like this. And I'm curious, ___? Let me know in the comments."

**Which to use:** Template A for big fantasy titles (lottery, $XM company, inheritance). Template B for realistic, relatable titles (portfolio, retired at 40, paid off house, 7 figures quietly).

Rules:
- Always give an **odd, exact time** (11:47, 7:41, 12:41, 6:47, 8:47). Never "one morning".
- Always include **a dollar amount in the first 15 seconds**.
- State the **secret within 30–60 seconds**: "Nobody here knows that. Nobody's supposed to." / "You don't tell anyone."

⚠️ **Viewers have noticed the repetition.** Comments say *"These AI generated scripts always consider Tuesday the most mundane day of the week 😂"* and *"It's always 6:22 on a Friday morning. Or 7:14 on a Thursday morning."* [COMMENTS] **Vary the day, the place, and the device** so your channel doesn't feel templated.

### 1.4 Body mechanics [DATA]
- **Time jumps:** one every 300–450 words in Template A, or a spoken section label every 300–450 words in Template B
- **Contrast sentences** carry the theme: "Your friends thought you were making 45,000 a year. In reality, you were taking home nearly 140,000."
- **Setting details** come from the same palette: burnt coffee, fluorescent light, a cracked phone screen, a 7-year-old sedan, Tupperware, the kettle clicking off, a rainy Tuesday in November
- **The finance lesson is told, then compressed into one plain line:** "$75 a week is $3,900 a year… But $3,900 invested is different from $3,900 spent. And that difference is the entire story that follows."
- **Midpoint conflict** at 45–60%: a layoff announcement, a nosy friend (Dana), a market dip, a family member asking for money
- **The emotional cost** (60–80%): lying to family, loneliness, "money like this isn't freedom, it's a cage with very soft walls"
- **The quiet win** (80–95%): a two-sentence resignation letter, paying off mom's mortgage anonymously, helping a brother with a $6,000 roof

### 1.5 Phrase bank (the house voice) [DATA]
Use each **once at most per script**:
- "Nobody here knows that. Nobody's supposed to."
- "That's the part nobody tells you about money."
- "From the outside, ___. Underneath, ___."
- "It wasn't ___. It was ___."
- "That was rule one, and you wrote it down that same week."
- "You didn't post about it. You didn't tell your friends."
- "What looked like a person standing still was actually a person who had already left."
- "The best kind of freedom isn't the kind you show the world. It's the kind only you know exists."
- "You don't have to look rich to be free."

### 1.6 Endings [DATA]
- **Template A:** a dilemma as an either/or question. No CTA. This makes people argue in the comments; the lottery video has 568 comments, mostly people saying what *they* would do.
- **Template B:** a bookend scene → "If this story made you ___, hit like and subscribe for more stories like this. And I'm curious, ___? Let me know in the comments. Thanks for watching."

### 1.7 What the comments tell you [COMMENTS]
- The lottery video's top comments are **"what I would do" fantasies**: "No way I'm picking up another job after having that much money" (505 likes), "I ain't getting a job or eating gas station sandwich". **Design the ending so viewers argue with the protagonist's choices.**
- Practical tips in comments, like "Set up anonymous annuities for everyone you care about", show that viewers come for real finance knowledge. Keep the concepts accurate.
- Viewers notice AI patterns (the Tuesday joke). Human-edit every script.
- **Comment-section growth hack in the niche:** many top comments on Ollie's videos come from *other small POV channels* (@MoneyMotionPOV, @wealthfinancePOV, @FinanceInfoPOV, @WealthyMath) posting generic praise to get profile clicks. Use this lightly, with real, thoughtful comments, not spam.

## 2. VOICEOVER

### 2.1 Measured pacing [DATA]
| Spec | Target |
|---|---|
| Speed | **155–162 wpm** (the winners' range). Don't go above 165. |
| Words in the first 60 s | 155–185 |
| Pause after a punch line (≤5 words) | 0.5–0.8 s |
| Pause after a time-jump/section label | 0.8–1.2 s, and change the visual + SFX here |
| Final question | Slow down to ~140 wpm, then 2 s of silence before the end screen |

### 2.2 Voice character [INFERRED]
I couldn't verify gender, age, or whether the voice is AI or human. The scripts are clearly AI-assisted [COMMENTS], and channels in this niche almost always use a **premium AI narrator** (ElevenLabs-type).

Recommended profile:
- Calm, warm, low-to-mid pitch American narrator, sounding late 30s to 40s, like a documentary narrator who is "telling you a secret"
- Emotion range: mostly restrained; slightly more tension in conflict beats; softer and slower at the ending
- **Not** hype, not "YouTuber" energy

**ElevenLabs starting settings** (tune by ear):
| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Model | latest multilingual/English high-quality model |
| Stability | 45–55% (lower sounds more emotional but less consistent) |
| Similarity | 75–85% |
| Style exaggeration | 0–20% |
| Speed | 0.95–1.0 (aim for ~158 wpm measured) |
| Generation | in chunks of 300–600 words; keep the same settings for every chunk |

**Script markup for the TTS:**
- New paragraph = natural pause. Put each punch line on its own line.
- Use `—` or `...` for a mid-sentence breath. Don't overuse it.
- Write numbers the way they should be spoken where TTS gets confused: "$1,140,000" → "one million, one hundred forty thousand dollars"; "2016 Corolla" → "twenty sixteen Corolla"
- Avoid ALL CAPS (some engines shout it)

**Audio post-processing:** high-pass at 80 Hz → light de-ess → compressor (3:1) → limiter → **−14 LUFS integrated, −1 dBTP**. Add 0.3 s of room tone at the head.

⏸ Your own voice choice and A/B testing come later. Test 2 voices on the same script and compare retention at 30 s.

### 3.2 Most likely visual system [INFERRED]
**Illustrated story scenes, one per spoken idea.**

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Style | 2D digital illustration / semi-flat cartoon, cinematic lighting, muted palette + warm accent |
| Main character | One recurring, generic, faceless or simple-faced protagonist (hoodie/work shirt), consistent across the video |
| Visuals per minute | **8–12** (one change every 5–7.5 s at 160 wpm) |
| Visuals per video | 14 min ≈ **120–170**; 21 min ≈ **170–250** |
| First 30 s | Faster: a new image every 2–3 s (10–15 images) |
| Motion | Slow Ken Burns zoom (100→108%) or pan on every image; alternate directions |
| Extra motion | Occasional animated loops on key beats (blink, steam from coffee, phone buzz shake, rain on window) |
| Transitions | Hard cuts on sentences; a cross-dissolve or whoosh on time jumps |
| Data visuals | Simple on-screen graphics when money is spoken: balance counters, a phone banking UI, a line chart crossing ("portfolio income vs salary") |

**Alternative B (if your manual check shows realistic footage):** AI-generated cinematic photorealistic stills or stock footage (Storyblocks/Pexels) of offices, apartments, and cash. The rest of the spec stays the same.

### 3.3 Character & style bible (fill this in once, reuse forever)
```
STYLE: 2D digital illustration, cinematic composition, soft volumetric light, muted
navy/charcoal/beige palette with a single warm gold accent, clean outlines,
subtle film grain, 16:9, no text.
PROTAGONIST: man (or woman) early 30s, short dark hair, plain gray hoodie or
light-blue work shirt, average build, face partly in shadow / seen from side or behind.
RECURRING PROPS: cracked-screen phone, old silver sedan, laptop with glowing
dashboard, Tupperware lunch, coffee mug.
SIDE CHARACTERS: drawn in the same style, slightly desaturated.
```
Prompt pattern for each scene:
```
[STYLE]. [PROTAGONIST] [action], [location + time-of-day lighting], [one emotional detail],
[camera: wide/medium/close-up/over-the-shoulder], no text.
```
Example (Lottery 0:00–0:10):
1. `[STYLE] Close-up of a card reader screen flashing "DECLINED" on a gas station counter at night, fluorescent green light, no text other than the screen.`
2. `[STYLE] [PROTAGONIST] standing at a gas station register at 11:47 PM, cashier looking annoyed, harsh fluorescent light, medium shot.`
3. `[STYLE] Hands digging coins out of a worn wallet, a crumpled lottery ticket visible in the corner, extreme close-up.`
4. `[STYLE] Phone screen in hand showing lottery numbers, ticket held next to it, glow lighting the fingers, close-up.`

Tools: Midjourney (`--sref` / `--cref` for consistency), Leonardo (character reference), ChatGPT image, Ideogram, Flux. For animated loops: Kling / Runway / Luma image-to-video (3–5 s clips) on 5–10 key scenes only.

## 4. MUSIC & SOUND DESIGN [INFERRED]

| Layer | Spec |
|---|---|
| Music bed | Soft cinematic piano / ambient / emotional lo-fi. 70–90 BPM, minor or neutral key. |
| Level | −24 to −28 dB under the voice (the voice always clear); duck automatically |
| Music changes | 3–4 tracks per video: (1) intro mystery/tension, (2) building/hopeful during the "boring years", (3) darker for the conflict at ~50%, (4) warm and resolved for the ending |
| SFX | Phone buzz/notification, cash register/coins on money reveals, whoosh on time jumps, a soft riser before reveals, keyboard typing, ambient room tone (office hum, rain, city night) |
| SFX level | Subtle: −18 to −22 dB. They should be *felt*, not heard. |
| Sources | YouTube Audio Library (free), Epidemic Sound, Artlist, Pixabay Music. Avoid unlicensed tracks (Content ID). |

## 5. EDITING: TIMELINE BUILD

**Track layout (CapCut / Premiere / DaVinci):**
```
V4  Subtitles (auto-captions, styled)
V3  Text pop-ups / time-jump cards / UI mockups
V2  Overlays: film grain 5–10%, vignette, light leaks on transitions (optional)
V1  Images/clips (Ken Burns on each)
A1  Voiceover
A2  Music (ducked)
A3  SFX
A4  Room tone/ambience
```

**Step-by-step:**
1. Drop the full voiceover on A1. Cut out breaths and gaps over 0.8 s (except intentional pauses).
2. Place markers at every sentence end, time jump, and number.
3. Lay images on V1 to the markers, about 5–7 s each (2–3 s in the first 30 s and in tense beats).
4. Apply a Ken Burns preset to every still (batch preset), alternating zoom-in and zoom-out.
5. Add money pop-ups, time-jump cards, and UI mockups (V3).
6. Add the music bed, split at the 4 emotional turns, ducked under the voice.
7. Add SFX at reveals, time jumps, and notifications.
8. Auto-generate captions, apply the caption style template, and fix any wrong words (numbers especially).
9. Overlays: grain + vignette at low opacity.
10. End screen: last 20 s, 2 videos + subscribe.
11. Export: 1080p (or 4K upscale), 30 fps, H.264, 12–16 Mbps, audio at −14 LUFS.

**Retention rules:**
- 0:00–0:30: no logo, no intro, no "welcome back". A new visual every 2–3 s.
- Never hold one image longer than 8 s.
- Every 60–90 s, add a pattern break: a UI mockup, a number pop-up, a time-jump card, or a music change.
- Payoff the title promise before 1:00.
